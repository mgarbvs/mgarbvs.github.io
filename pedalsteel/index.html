Pedal Steel

A 10-string E9 pedal steel for Ableton Live that you can play without ever having touched one.

Play chords on a keyboard and it voices them the way a steel player would: one bar position plus pedals and knee levers. Change chords legato and the strings bend (pedals) or slide (bar) into the new chord instead of being picked again.

Three Max for Live devices share the same player: PedalSteel synthesizes the strings, SampleSteel plays any sample you drop in, and InputSteel turns the audio on a track into the steel.

Play it in your browser   View on GitHub

Requires Ableton Live Suite with Max for Live. Copy the m4l/ folder somewhere (the .js files must stay next to the .amxd files) and drag a device onto a track.

The devices

Jump to: PedalSteel | SampleSteel | InputSteel | Push 3 | Playing | How it works

Each demo plays the same phrase: E major, then A major held legato (a pedal bend), then a slide up to C. All three were rendered from the devices' own code.

PedalSteel

The instrument. Ten Karplus-Strong strings that can be retuned while they ring, a single-coil pickup, a clean amp and a spring reverb. Bend In picks some chords unpedaled and presses the pedals about 90 ms after the strum, for the steel's “cry”.

Click a pedal on the display to latch it. Outlined: pressed by the voicing for this chord. Solid: latched by you.

SampleSteel

Any sound as a pedal steel. The same player, with the ten strings replaced by ten voices playing a sample you drop in. Set Root to the sample's note; held notes loop the Start–End section with an equal-power crossfade, so they sustain and bend as long as you hold them. Turn Loop off for plucked or struck sounds.

The sample is a synthetic bowed tone at C3, the one in the screenshot.

InputSteel

Your instrument as a pedal steel. An audio effect: sing or play one note into the track, hold chords on a keyboard (picked in MIDI From), and each of the ten voices pitch-shifts your sound to its string's note, so it bends and slides into the chords. Dry mixes the untouched input back in.

The input is a synthetic sung “ah” held on A3. In Live, Max's fzero~ tracks its pitch; here the render is given the true pitch.

On Push 3

On Push the encoders page through Play, Sound and Pedals (SampleSteel adds Sample). The screen is drawn in Live 13's flat style: Play shows the neck with the ringing strings lit, Pedals shows one pedal per encoder with the notes it changes and what the ten strings sound now, and Sample shows the waveform with the loop section lit.

In both modes pitch bend slides the bar ±2 frets, the mod wheel adds vibrato, CC 64 sustains and CC 11 is the volume pedal. With MPE (Push 3, Seaboard, Linnstrument) each note's pitch bend bends just its string, pressure swells it and slide makes it brighter or darker.

How it works

  • Real E9 tuning. Ten strings (F#4 D#4 G#4 E4 B3 G#3 F#3 E3 D3 B2) with the standard A/B/C pedals and four knee levers.
  • Voicing like a player. Every chord is scored across 23 bar positions × 96 pedal combinations a player can physically press. The score prefers the melody on top, compact grips, few pedals, small bar moves and, above all, keeping ringing strings ringing.
  • Pitch that moves while a string rings. Each string is a delay line one period long; its length follows a pitch ramp, so a pedal or bar move stretches the ringing string instead of starting a new note. Every string stays within ±1 cent of its target.
  • The rest of the steel. A pick position and a pickup that comb-filter each string, sympathetic resonance between strings, 1 cent of cabinet drop per raise pedal, a bar vibrato that fades in after the pick, and the amp's spring reverb.

The player is steel_brain.js, the sound is a gen~ codebox, and the browser version runs both unchanged.
